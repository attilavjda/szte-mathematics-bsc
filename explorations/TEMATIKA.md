# Kalkulus I tárgytematika ↔ this library's proof

*A categorical dictionary.*

The uploaded syllabus lists the topics of a BSc Calculus I course.  This
document matches each item against a component of the proof developed in this
repository — directly, or as a **dual**, an **isomorphic copy**, or the **same
statement read in an equivalent context** — and states each correspondence in
categorical language.  Every entry marked ✅ is backed by Lean theorems in
`Tematika/`, in which the Calculus I side (over `ℝ`, from Mathlib) and the
library side (over the finite ground object, from `Atlas/` and `Genesis/`) are
both proved, so that the correspondence is a *pair of theorems* and not an
analogy.  Entries marked ○ are discussed but not formalised.

## 0. The two contexts, and the comparison between them

Calculus I is done in the context

> `(ℝ, order topology, colimits along neighbourhood filters)`

and the library's proof in the context

> `(K a finite field of order 2ⁿ, discrete topology, finite sums over the group)`.

The dictionary between them is a systematic change of site:

| Calculus I | library | categorical reason |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| colimit along the filter of punctured neighbourhoods (`lim`) | sum over the whole group (`∑ x : K`) | both are the universal cocone of the diagram of "local data"; over a discrete finite object the filter is principal and the colimit is a finite (co)product |
| compactness of `[a, b]` | finiteness of `K` | a finite discrete space is compact — the *same* theorem then applies, see `Tematika.Continuity.exists_max_of_finite_discrete` |
| connectedness of `[a, b]` | *absent* | a discrete space is totally disconnected; the intermediate value theorem survives only in its unit-step form |
| the derivative `lim_{h→0} (f(x+h) − f(x))/h` | the difference operator `D_a f = f(·+a) − f` | `D_a = T_a^* − id` is the deviation of the translation action from the identity; over `ℝ` one further divides and takes the colimit, over `K` there is nothing to divide by |
| positivity in the ordered field `ℝ` | positivity of real parts inside `ℝ ⊆ ℂ` | the ordered structure is transported along the unique embedding, and this is where the library's proof becomes an analysis argument |

Everything below is an instance of this table.

## 1. A valós számtest ✅ `Tematika/Ground.lean`

**Categorical form.**  A ground object is singled out by a universal property,
not chosen.  `ℝ` is the conditionally complete linearly ordered field, unique
**up to a unique isomorphism** (`real_unique_up_to_unique_iso`).

**Library component.**  The finite field `K` with `#K = 2ⁿ`, unique up to
isomorphism by cardinality (`finite_field_unique_of_card`) — but *not* uniquely
so: the comparison is a torsor under the Galois group generated by the
Frobenius.  That surplus automorphism is precisely what the proof exploits
(`Genesis/Counting/IteratedFrobeniusAutomorphism.lean`) and what Calculus I does
not have.

**Common base.**  Both objects sit under the initial object `ℤ` of
`CommRingCat` (`int_isInitial`, `subsingleton_ringHom_int`): the arithmetic
identities of the two courses are the image of one arithmetic under the two
unique structure maps.

## 2. Teljes indukció ✅ `Tematika/Induction.lean`

**Categorical form.**  `ℕ` with `(0, succ)` is the **initial algebra of the
endofunctor `X ↦ 1 ⊕ X`** (Lambek).  Recursion is existence of the mediating
morphism, induction is its uniqueness: `nat_isInitialAlgebra`.

**Library component.**  The Frobenius tower.  The algebra is
`(K →+* K, id, frobenius ∘ −)`, and the mediating morphism out of `ℕ` is
`iterateFrobenius` (`iterateFrobenius_unique`).  Every propagation of a fact
from `x ↦ x²` to `x ↦ x^(2^k)` in the library is that one morphism.

## 3. Nevezetes egyenlőtlenségek ✅ `Tematika/Inequalities.lean`

**Categorical form.**  An inequality is a morphism in the poset `ℝ`; the
classical inequalities come from positivity of a quadratic form and from the
dagger (inner product/adjoint) structure of the function space.

**Library component.**  Parseval — the transform is an isometry up to `|K|`
(`Atlas.parseval`) — and the nonnegativity `0 ≤ ‖z‖²` that makes the third
moment nonnegative.  AM–GM and Cauchy–Schwarz are recorded on the Calculus I
side as the same positivity.

## 4. Polinomok, racionális törtfüggvények, gyökös, trigonometrikus, exponenciális függvények és inverzeik ✅ `Tematika/FunctionZoo.lean`

Each elementary function is an object determined by a functional equation; the
library's zoo is that equation read over `K`:

| Calculus I | equation | library |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| `exp` | `f(x+y) = f(x)·f(y)` | additive characters `ψ : K → ℂ` — a homomorphism of groups, i.e. a functor between one-object groupoids (`real_exp_hom`, `addChar_hom`) |
| `cos nθ` | `T_n(cos θ) = cos nθ` | **Dickson polynomials** (`Genesis/Dickson/`): the *same polynomial* satisfies `D_n(2 cos θ) = 2 cos nθ` over `ℝ` (`dickson_eval_two_cos`) and `D_n(x + x⁻¹) = xⁿ + x⁻ⁿ` over any commutative ring (`dickson_eval_add_inv`).  The change of context is the parametrisation `x + x⁻¹ ↦ 2 cos θ` of the norm-one torus |
| `√` | inverse of `x ↦ x²` | the **inverse Frobenius**: in characteristic two squaring is bijective, so the square root is an automorphism, not a partial inverse (`sq_inverseFrobenius`, `inverseFrobenius_sq`) |
| polynomials | free algebra on one generator | the Kasami monomial `x ↦ x^(4^k − 2^k + 1)` |
| rational functions | localisation away from the zero locus | the slope `(x + z)/(y + z)`, defined off `y = z`; the admissible locus `ρ ∉ {0, 1}` is its értelmezési tartomány (`admissible_iff`) |

This row is the sharpest instance of "iso in an equivalent context": the
trigonometric identities of Calculus I and the Dickson identities of the proof
are the same identities in `ℤ[x]`.

## 5. Értelmezési tartomány, értékkészlet, inverz függvény, összetétel ✅ `Tematika/DomainRange.lean`

**Categorical form.**  The four primitives of a category read in **Set**:
source and target, composition, isomorphism (`iso_iff_bijective`), and the
**(epi, mono) factorisation** `α ↠ im f ↪ β`, whose middle object is unique up
to a unique isomorphism (`exists_image_factorisation`,
`image_factorisation_unique`).

**Library component.**  The set `Δ` that the entire counting argument is about
is the image object of the derivative map (`derivativeImage_is_image`); the
proof never mentions the derivative again, only its értékkészlet.  Dually, the
*fibres* of the slope map (its level sets) are what the count is fibred over
(`Atlas/Count/Fibration.lean`) — image and fibre are the coimage/kernel pair of
the same morphism.

## 6. Grafikonok vázolása, szimmetria tulajdonságok, monotonitás, elemi függvénytranszformációk ✅ `Tematika/Transformations.lean`

* **Grafikon** — the graph is the subobject of `α × β` on which `pr₁` is an
  isomorphism (`graph_proj_bijective`): "a function is its graph".
* **Elemi függvénytranszformációk** — shift, stretch and reflect form a group;
  a quantity is transformation-invariant when it factors through the orbit
  category.  In the library this is a single brick,
  `Atlas.tripleCount_transport`, whose instances are scaling, translation and
  ring automorphism; their affine combination is `tripleCount_affine`.
* **Szimmetria** — the `S₃`-action permuting the three coordinates
  (`Atlas.tripleCount_swap₁₂/₂₃`) and the **anharmonic (cross-ratio) group** of
  order six acting on the slope line by `ρ ↦ ρ⁻¹` and `ρ ↦ 1 + ρ`
  (`Atlas.slopeCount_inv`, `Atlas.slopeCount_one_add`); its order-three element
  is `anharmonic_cube`.  Calculus I meets the same group as the symmetries
  `x ↦ 1/x`, `x ↦ 1 − x` of a graph.
* **Monotonitás** — a monotone map *is* a functor between posets.  The count is
  monotone in its set argument, hence a functor `Set M ⥤ ℕ`
  (`tripleCount_mono`, `tripleCountFunctor`).

## 7. Függvények határértéke, formális tulajdonságai, műveletek ✅ `Tematika/Limits.lean`

* **Uniqueness of the limit** ↦ **uniqueness of the invariant functional**: over
  a finite group, every translation-invariant linear functional is a multiple of
  the total sum (`invariant_functional_eq_smul_sum`); invariance itself is
  `sum_translate`.  Both are the statement that a universal object is unique.
* **Műveletek** ("the limit of a product is the product of the limits") ↦
  **monoidality of the duality functor**: the Fourier transform takes
  convolution to the pointwise product (`transform_conv_eq_mul`, from
  `Atlas.transform_conv`), the unit `δ₀` to the unit `1` (`transform_delta`,
  `conv_delta`), and sums to sums (`transform_add`).  The library's entire
  counting identity is this: the count is a triple convolution at `0`, hence a
  triple product frequency by frequency
  (`Atlas.tripleConv_zero_eq_transform_average`).
* **The duality is an involution** — Pontryagin duality for finite abelian
  groups (`doubleDual_bijective`), the structural reason Fourier inversion
  (`Atlas.inversion_at_zero`) exists.  This is "inverz függvény" one level up:
  an equivalence of categories rather than a bijection of sets.

## 8. Folytonosság, középérték-tétel, kompakt intervallumon folytonos függvények ✅ `Tematika/Continuity.lean`

* **Folytonosság** is being a morphism in `Top`.  Over a discrete ground object
  every map is continuous (`continuous_of_discrete`), so continuity carries no
  information and the working notion of morphism drops to the algebraic level —
  which is why the library's transport statements are about additive and ring
  maps.
* **Weierstrass** transports *literally*: a finite discrete space is compact, so
  Mathlib's compactness extremum theorem applies as it stands
  (`exists_max_of_finite_discrete`).  The library's "compact interval" is the
  finite admissible slope set `Atlas.slopeSet K`, on which the count attains its
  maximum (`exists_max_slopeCount`).
* **Középérték-tétel (Bolzano)** does not transport, because connectedness
  fails; its surviving form is the unit-step version
  (`discrete_intermediate_value`): a quantity whose increments are at most one
  and which passes a level must hit it.

**Dual pair.**  Weierstrass is a *compactness* (limit-side) statement and
Bolzano a *connectedness* (colimit-side) statement; the discretisation keeps the
first and breaks the second, which is exactly why a counting proof replaces
"there is a point where the value is attained" by "the average is attained".

## 9. Pontbeli derivált, érintőegyenes, deriválási szabályok, elemi függvények deriváltja, láncszabály, implicit deriválás ✅ `Tematika/Derivative.lean`

**Categorical form.**  `D_a = T_a^* − id`: the deviation from the identity of
the translation action `a ↦ T_a^*`, a functor from the one-object groupoid
`(K, +)` into `End(K → K)`.

Everything Calculus I proves about `d/dx` that does not use the limit holds
verbatim: linearity (`diff_add`), Leibniz (`diff_mul`), the **chain rule**
(`diff_comp`: `D_a(g∘f)(x) = D_{D_a f(x)} g (f x)`), **Clairaut symmetry** of
mixed second derivatives (`diff_diff_comm`), "the derivative of a linear map is
its slope" (`diff_addMonoidHom`), and the elementary computation
`D_a(x ↦ x²) = a²` in characteristic two (`diff_sq`).

**Library component — this is the central correspondence.**

* The set `Δ` the whole proof is about is the image of a derivative:
  `Genesis.Kasami.kasamiDerivative` is `D₁` of the Kasami power map, normalised
  (`kasamiDerivative_eq_diff`).
* The half-size input is a **second derivative test**: `D_t D₁ F = 0` says the
  first derivative is `t`-periodic, and with no further coincidences the
  derivative is two-to-one, so its image is exactly half of `K`
  (`isHalf_image_of_secondDiff_vanishing`, i.e.
  `Atlas.isHalf_image_of_kernel_pair` read as a second-derivative statement).
* The library's slope is Calculus I's **difference quotient**:
  `Atlas.slopeOfTriple x y z = (x − z)/(y − z)`
  (`slopeOfTriple_eq_difference_quotient`).
* **Érintőegyenes** is double contact, and that is algebraic: `a` is a double
  root of `P` exactly when `P(a) = P'(a) = 0` (`sq_X_sub_C_dvd_iff`).

○ **Implicit deriválás and the tangent line in the curve half.**  The curve
part of the library (`Genesis/Curve/`) is built on the secant-and-tangent group
law: `SecantSlopeXCoordinate.lean` is the secant slope, the tangent case is
detected by a vanishing denominator
(`VanishingNonvanishingHessianDenominators.lean`,
`ThreeTorsionVanishingDenominator.lean`), and a vanishing denominator in the
implicitly differentiated curve equation is exactly Calculus I's "vertical
tangent".  Points at infinity (`PointsAtInfinityAreThreeTorsion.lean`) play the
role of asymptotes in curve sketching.  These are stated here as
correspondences, not re-proved.

## 10–14. Középérték-tételek; monotonitás és derivált; lokális szélsőérték; konvexitás; grafikon vázolása ✅ `Tematika/Extrema.lean`

**Categorical form.**  An extremum is a **(co)limit in a poset**, and the whole
block is one pattern:

> a monotone functor between posets whose two endpoint values agree is constant
> (`const_of_monotoneOn_endpoints`),

with the derivative hypotheses serving only to produce the monotonicity.

* **Calculus I instance** — monotonicity from `f' ≥ 0` (the mean value
  theorem), constancy from equal endpoints
  (`const_of_deriv_nonneg_of_endpoints`); Fermat's stationary condition
  (`deriv_eq_zero_of_isLocalMax`); convexity from `f'' ≥ 0`
  (`convexOn_of_deriv2_nonneg_real`), the archetype being `x ↦ x²`
  (`convexOn_sq`).
* **Library instance** — each slope count is `main term + correction/|K|`, every
  correction is nonnegative (a `‖·‖²`, `normSq_nonneg_re`), and the average over
  the admissible slopes is *exactly* the main term.  The same order-theoretic
  pattern then forces every correction to vanish (`const_of_mean_of_ge`, the
  arithmetic heart of `Atlas.forcing_of_average_and_nonneg`), which is what
  `Atlas.slopeCount_eq_of_moment_nonneg` runs on
  (`slopeCount_const_of_moment_nonneg`).

**This is the point at which the library's proof *is* a calculus argument**: an
inequality together with an exactly matching average is an equality case — "the
function attains its minimum everywhere, so its derivative vanishes
identically".  The library's "grafikon vázolása" is the resulting picture: the
slope-count function on the admissible slope line is constant, equal to
`|K|²/8`.

**Dual pair.**  The first derivative test for a maximum and for a minimum are
the same statement in `P` and in `Pᵒᵖ`; the library uses the pair
simultaneously — the counts are bounded below by the mean and average to the
mean, so they are both minimal and maximal.

## 15. L'Hospital szabályok és alkalmazásaik ✅ `Tematika/Hospital.lean`

**Categorical form.**  A `0/0` expression is a quotient not determined by its
zeroth-order data; the answer is read off the next layer of the filtration —
the associated graded piece.

* **The indeterminate form in the library.**  On the degenerate locus the
  difference quotient has vanishing numerator and denominator, and there
  *every* value of `ρ` satisfies the relation (`slope_indeterminate`): `0/0` in
  its literal meaning.  The library therefore never divides there; the locus is
  handled by a separate lemma (`Atlas.mem_solutions_slope_iff_of_eq`), the
  counting analogue of "differentiate numerator and denominator".
* **The resolution.**  Every count has the same leading term `c`, so all the
  content is in the first-order term, recovered as the rescaled difference
  `(N − c)·|K| = Z` (`first_order_term`); the positivity of that first-order
  term is what closes the argument.
* For comparison, the analytic rule itself is applied in one classical
  instance: `sin x / x → 1` (`tendsto_sin_div_atZero`).

## Summary of the correspondence types

| type | examples |
| :-- | :-- |
| **the same theorem, applied in the finite context** | Weierstrass (compactness), the order-theoretic mean value pattern, Cauchy–Schwarz/positivity |
| **isomorphic objects in equivalent contexts** | Dickson ≅ Chebyshev; `exp` ≅ additive character; `√` ≅ inverse Frobenius; ℕ-recursion ≅ the Frobenius tower |
| **dual** | image ↔ fibre (coimage/kernel); max ↔ min (`P` vs `Pᵒᵖ`); convolution ↔ pointwise product under the duality functor; compactness (Weierstrass) ↔ connectedness (Bolzano) |
| **survives only in weakened form** | the intermediate value theorem (unit-step version); the derivative (difference operator, no division by `h`) |
| **absent on one side** | the Frobenius automorphism (no `ℝ` counterpart); connectedness (no `K` counterpart) |
