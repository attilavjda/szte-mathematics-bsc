# The Darboux Gadget — a guide to the big ideas

*Companion to `RequestProject/Gadgets/DarbouxGadget.lean` and
`RequestProject/Gadgets/DarbouxCharTwo.lean`.  An illustrated expository version with
diagrams is in `docs/darboux-gadget.tex` / `docs/darboux-gadget.pdf`.*

---

## 0. The one-sentence version

Mathlib's Darboux theorem — *the image of a derivative is an interval* — and the
Kasami/Gold statement in this library — *the image of a discrete derivative is an affine
subspace* — are **the same theorem about a different convexity structure**, and this file
family makes that literal: one abstract gadget, several instantiations, and machine-checked
transports between them.

---

## 1. What the gadget is

A **convexity structure** (`Gadget.Convexity`) on a type `β` is nothing but a family of
subsets called *convex*, containing `univ` and closed under arbitrary intersections — a
closure system.  That is the minimum needed to say "the image of the derivative is a nice
connected piece" without committing to order, topology or linearity.

```lean
def HasDarbouxOn (C : Convexity β) (Adm : (α → β) → Prop) (D : (α → β) → α → β) : Prop :=
  ∀ f, Adm f → C.IsConvex (Set.range (D f))
```

Three data determine a Darboux-type theorem:

| slot | real analysis | characteristic two |
|---|---|---|
| operator `D` | `deriv` | `D a f x = f (x + a) - f x` |
| admissible class `Adm` | differentiable | quadratic (constant second derivative) |
| convexity `C` | order-connected sets (`Set.OrdConnected`) | affine sets, closed under `u - v + w` |

* `Gadget.real_hasDarboux` — Mathlib's `Set.OrdConnected.image_deriv`, repackaged.
* `Gadget.darboux_of_constIncrements` — the characteristic-two theorem, proved once and for
  all in three lines for *any* abelian groups.
* `Gadget.gold_darboux` — the Gold power map `x ↦ x^(2^k+1)` satisfies the hypothesis, so
  the Kasami-library statement `Tematika.Kalkulus.gold_deriv_affine_closed` becomes a
  *corollary of a context-free lemma*.  This is the factoring the title promises: the
  cryptographic computation now only verifies a hypothesis; the geometry is generic.

### Cool fact 1 — the proof is a translation trick

Real Darboux needs the mean value theorem and a compactness/IVT argument.  Its
characteristic-two shadow needs one line: if the increment `g(x + b) - g(x)` does not depend
on the base point `x`, then

$$g(x) - g(y) + g(z) = g\bigl(z + (x - y)\bigr).$$

The witness for the affine combination of three values is an affine combination of three
*arguments*.  Nothing else is used.

### Cool fact 2 — "interval" and "coset" are the same classification theorem

Over `ℝ`: a nonempty order-connected set is an interval.
In an abelian group: a nonempty set closed under `u - v + w` is a **coset of a subgroup**
(`Gadget.isAffine_iff_coset`).  The operation `u - v + w` is the ternary *heap* (torsor)
operation; the classification is the standard "heap = coset" theorem, and it is exactly
what "the derivative image is a discrete interval" means.

## 2. Faithful and non-faithful contexts

A convexity structure is **faithful** (`Gadget.Convexity.Faithful`) if *some* set fails to be
convex.  Only then does the Darboux property say anything.

* `faithful_ordConvexity_real`: `{0, 1} ⊆ ℝ` is not an interval — the real statement has
  content.
* `faithful_affineConvexity`: `{0, e₁, e₂} ⊆ F₂³` is not affine — the characteristic-two
  statement has content.
* `Convexity.not_faithful_indiscrete` + `hasDarbouxOn_indiscrete`: in the *indiscrete*
  structure every set is convex, so **every** operator has the Darboux property.  This is
  the exact shape of a non-faithful functor: the theorem survives, its content does not.

This is the same phenomenon the library already records elsewhere:
`Tematika.Kalkulus.continuity_not_faithful` (on a finite set every map is continuous, so
"continuous" separates nothing) and `Tematika.Morita.diffUnif_not_faithful`.  The reason
the real Darboux theorem cannot be *transported literally* to `GF(2ⁿ)` is
`Tematika.Morita.no_linear_order_of_charTwo`: characteristic two admits no compatible linear
order, so the order convexity structure has no image there.  What survives the passage is
the *affine* structure — order is lost, the heap operation is not.

### Cool fact 3 — the loss is exactly one bit of structure

Order-convexity is generated by *pairs* (`x ≤ z ≤ y`); affine convexity is generated by
*triples* (`u - v + w`).  Going from `ℝ` to `GF(2ⁿ)` you give up betweenness and keep
mid-point/heap symmetry.  Both are closure systems; only the arity of the generating
operation changes.

## 3. Morita transport: the same theorem in two pictures

`GF(2ⁿ)` is an `F₂`-progenerator, and `Tematika.Morita.endAlgEquivMatrix` identifies
`End_{F₂}(GF(2ⁿ)) ≅ M_n(F₂)`.  Under the corresponding coordinate equivalence
`coordEquiv : GF(2ⁿ) ≃ₗ F₂ⁿ`:

* `Gadget.coord_gold_deriv` — the Gold derivative becomes the affine matrix map
  `v ↦ M ⬝ v + c`, with `M` the matrix of the cross form `Cross k a`;
* `Gadget.gold_darboux_iff_matrix_darboux` — the two Darboux statements are **equivalent**,
  proved as an `iff`, not asserted;
* `Gadget.hasDarbouxOn_conj_iff` — the abstract reason: the whole gadget is stable under
  conjugation by equivalences of the domain and the codomain, together with transport of the
  convexity structure (`Convexity.map`).

So "polynomial picture" (linearised polynomials over the field) and "matrix picture"
(`n × n` matrices over `F₂`) are two readings of one statement, and the library now contains
the verification rather than the slogan.

## 4. Where the gadget already appears across the library

| occurrence | statement | Lean |
|---|---|---|
| Gold derivative | image is a hyperplane coset, `2^(n-1)` elements | `Gadget.gold_darboux_coset`, `Tematika.Kalkulus.card_gold_deriv_range` |
| Artin–Schreier map `x ↦ x^(2^k) + x` | image is exactly the trace hyperplane | `Gadget.range_LK_eq_hyperplane`, `Tematika.LinAlg.L_solvable_iff_trace_zero` |
| APN functions (measure form) | every nonzero derivative image has `|F|/2` elements | `Gadget.apn_derivRange_card`, `Papers.Dobbertin.two_mul_card_derivImage` |
| fast points (degree context) | fast points ∪ {0} form a subspace, hence affine | `Gadget.fastPoints_darboux`, `Notes.Freud.fastPointSubspace` |
| Rolle, the dual side | injectivity ⟺ no direction has a critical point | `Gadget.rolle_dual` |
| Walsh / Pontryagin dual | the derivative has a linear structure; character sum `± |F|` | `Gadget.gold_deriv_trace_const`, `Gadget.gold_deriv_char_sum` |
| matrix picture | affine matrix map `v ↦ M ⬝ v + c` | `Gadget.gold_darboux_iff_matrix_darboux` |

### Cool fact 4 — one number, `2^(n-1)`, three different reasons

The same cardinality shows up as: the size of a hyperplane (linear algebra), the size of the
image of a two-to-one map (counting/APN), and the number of `b` with `Tr(b) = 0`
(Artin–Schreier solvability).  In the Gold case all three coincide *by the same theorem*: the
image is a hyperplane coset.  In the Kasami case only the counting reason survives.

### Cool fact 5 — the affine form is strictly stronger than the counting form

Kasami exponents `2^(2k) - 2^k + 1` have algebraic degree `k + 1`, so for `k ≥ 2` the
functions are not quadratic and the hypothesis of the gadget fails.  Their derivative images
still have the right *size* — that is the APN property — but need not be affine.  This is
not hand-waving: `Gadget.measure_form_not_affine_form` exhibits a concrete derivative on
`F₂³` whose image has exactly `4 = 2^(3-1)` elements and is provably not affine (checked by
`decide`).  So:

```
affine (Darboux) form  ⟹  measure form,      measure form  ⇏  affine form.
```

Gold functions are the quadratic boundary case where the strong form holds; Kasami functions
are the interesting ones precisely because they achieve the counting conclusion *without* the
affine structure.

### Cool fact 6 — Darboux on the dual side is a "linear structure"

Because the Gold derivative's image is a coset of the hyperplane `Ker Tr(b · −)` with
`b = (N k a)⁻¹`, the map `x ↦ Tr(b · D_a f(x))` is **constant**
(`Gadget.gold_deriv_trace_const`), hence the character sum is extremal, `±|F|`
(`Gadget.gold_deriv_char_sum`).  Under the trace pairing — the Pontryagin self-duality that
mediates the APN ⟺ AB bridge in `RequestProject/Walsh` — "the image is small and flat"
becomes "some character sum is as large as possible".  Degeneracy on the primal side is
extremality on the dual side.

## 5. What all this achieves

1. **A reusable abstraction.**  `Convexity` + `HasDarbouxOn` is independent of the
   cryptographic setting; any future "image of an operator is structured" statement in the
   project can be phrased and transported with it.
2. **A genuine factoring of the Kasami/AB/APN development.**  The affine-image lemma is no
   longer a bespoke `char 2` computation; it is an instance, and the computation is confined
   to checking one hypothesis (constant increments).
3. **Verified comparisons rather than asserted analogies.**  Field ↔ coordinates ↔ matrices
   are related by proved `iff`s; faithfulness and non-faithfulness of the surrounding
   "functors" are proved statements, not commentary.
4. **An honest boundary.**  The counterexample fixes precisely how far the analogy reaches:
   Gold yes, Kasami only in measure.

## 6. Natural next steps

* **Higher-order Darboux.**  Iterated derivatives `D_a D_b f` and the degree filtration:
  a `k`-th order version of the gadget would put the Carlet–Feukoua–Sălăgean degree-stability
  results and the fast-point subspace into the same frame.
* **EA/CCZ-invariance of the gadget.**  Is "the derivative images are affine" preserved by
  extended-affine and CCZ equivalence?  The transport theorem is already the right shape for
  this question.
* **A convexity structure for the Walsh support.**  The dual-side occurrence above suggests a
  convexity structure on the spectrum in which the AB condition is itself a Darboux-type
  statement.
* **Ord-connected ↔ preconnected.**  Adding the topological instance (`IsPreconnected`) would
  give a third faithful context and make the "same theorem, three geometries" picture
  complete inside Lean.
